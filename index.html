    Formula

    The Poor Man's Bailout on a date t is how many days of printing, at the chosen average pace, it takes to equal the bitcoin market cap that day.

    s = t minus the window
N = calendar days from s to t
d(t) = C(t) / g(t)    when g(t) > 0

    C(t) is the bitcoin market cap in dollars. M(t) is the money stock in dollars, carried forward from the last print. The default pace is the simple trailing change:

    g(t) = (M(t) − M(s)) / N

    The other two paces, selected above:

    regression   g(t) = slope of daily M from s through t
CAGR pace    g(t) = M(t) × ((M(t) / M(s))^(1/N) − 1)

    The regression is an ordinary least-squares line on the forward-filled daily level, so the flat days between prints are part of the fit. The CAGR pace is the compound rate that links the level at the start of the window to today's level, stated in dollars per day at today's level. The default is the trailing change, on US M2, over 12 months.

    When g(t) is zero or negative — quantitative tightening, or the 2022–23 decline in M2 — d(t) is undefined. The chart leaves a gap. A negative or infinite day count is never drawn.

    How the series are aligned

    Prints arrive monthly, weekly, quarterly, or daily. Each money level is stepped forward to every calendar day and held until the next print. Bitcoin's market cap is the observation on that same calendar day, or the latest observation on or before it. The headline uses the later of the two last prints, and says when one of them was carried forward. Monthly figures taken from the Board's H.6 file are dated on the first of the month, which is how FRED dates the same print. Dollar levels in the snapshot are rounded to the nearest dollar.
