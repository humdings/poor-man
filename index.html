    Money Bitcoin

    The axiom

    Money spends the same, so expansion in any form counts as expansion. That includes M2, the monetary base, currency in circulation, the Fed balance sheet, and the public debt. Debt spends the same as cash. One person's debt is another's asset. Public debt stays in the comparison, on the same terms as the other measures.

    Formula

    The Poor Man's Bailout on a date t is how many days of monetary expansion, at the chosen pace, it takes to equal the bitcoin market cap that day.

    s = t minus the window
N = calendar days from s to t
d(t) = C(t) / g(t)    when g(t) > 0

    C(t) is the bitcoin market cap in dollars. M(t) is the monetary expansion measure in dollars, carried forward from the last print. The default pace is the simple trailing change, which is a 12-month smooth of US M2:

    g(t) = (M(t) − M(s)) / N

    The other two averaging methods:

    regression   g(t) = slope of daily M from s through t
CAGR pace    g(t) = M(t) × ((M(t) / M(s))^(1/N) − 1)

    The regression is an ordinary least-squares line on the forward-filled daily level, so the flat days between prints are part of the fit. The CAGR pace is the compound rate that links the level at the start of the window to today's level, stated in dollars per day at today's level. The default is the trailing change, on US M2, over 12 months.

    Smoothing

    The window and the averaging method are the smoothing. Several of each are above. None turns the smooth off and delivers the change as published:

    p = the print in effect on t
q = the print before p
g(t) = (M(p) − M(q)) / days from q to p

    That pace is held until the next print. If the print is lower than the one before it, g is negative for the whole stretch. The pace chart draws that decrease. The day count stays blank. A negative or infinite day count is never drawn, with or without smoothing. A longer window can hide a dip that a later rise reverses; with no smoothing, that dip stays a dip.

    When expansion stops

    When g(t) is zero or negative — quantitative tightening, the 2022–23 decline in M2, or a single print that falls — d(t) is undefined. The day-count chart leaves a gap. The pace chart keeps the decrease.

    What moved

    The change in the day count from the start of the window (or from the previous print, when smoothing is off) splits into three parts that add up. Money growth moves first, with bitcoin's price and the coins outstanding held at the reference date. The price moves next, coins still held. Coins outstanding move last.

    from money   = P(s)×S(s)/g(t) − P(s)×S(s)/g(s)
from price   = P(t)×S(s)/g(t) − P(s)×S(s)/g(t)
from coins   = P(t)×S(t)/g(t) − P(t)×S(s)/g(t)

    P is the bitcoin price and S is coins outstanding, market cap divided by price. Days at a fixed bitcoin price are P(s) × S(t) / g(t): today's coins and today's pace, at the reference price. Days at a fixed coin count are P(t) × S(s) / g(t). A longer bailout at a high price is the point. It is a higher valuation relative to monetary expansion.

    Normalizing for growth

    The checkbox is off by default. When it is on, the pace is money growth in excess of a deflator over the same interval the averaging method already uses. Nominal GDP is FRED GDP, quarterly, seasonally adjusted annual rate, in dollars. CPI is FRED CPIAUCSL, the monthly seasonally adjusted index. No API key. Only ratios enter the formula, so GDP's annualized rate cancels and CPI stays an index.

    trailing, and no smoothing
    g* = g − M(s) × (D(t) − D(s)) / (N × D(s))
CAGR
    r(X) = (X(t) / X(s))^(1/N) − 1
    g* = M(t) × (r(M) − r(D))
regression
    g* = slope(M) − M(t) × slope(ln D)

    Trailing and CAGR use the deflator at the two ends of the window. Regression uses every forward-filled day. A flat deflator leaves the pace unchanged. If the money stock grew at the same percentage rate as the deflator, the trailing and CAGR excess is zero and the day count pauses. The regression excess subtracts the deflator's daily log-slope, converted to dollars at today's level. If the deflator does not cover the interval, the day count is left blank rather than silently using the unadjusted pace.

    How the series are aligned

    Prints arrive monthly, weekly, quarterly, or daily. Each level — money, bitcoin market cap, bitcoin price, GDP, and CPI — is stepped forward to every calendar day and held until the next print. Bitcoin's market cap and price on a day are the observations on that calendar day, or the latest on or before it. The headline uses the later of the money series' last print and bitcoin's last print, and says when one of them was carried forward. Monthly figures taken from the Board's H.6 file are dated on the first of the month, which is how FRED dates the same print. Dollar levels in the snapshot are rounded to the nearest dollar. CPI is stored as the published index, including fractions of a point.
